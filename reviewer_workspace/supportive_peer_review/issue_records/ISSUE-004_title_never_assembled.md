# ISSUE-004 — The title phrase "Observer-Rooted Asymmetry" is never assembled in the body

**Severity: Level 3 (persuasiveness risk).** Classification: PERSUASIVENESS_RISK / READER-COMPREHENSION_RISK.

## Exact location
Title (sole occurrence of the phrase); §8.3 (the only body definition of the relevant asymmetry); §10.2 (designated reference cell); §12 ("rooted" projector/record).

## Exact claim under review
Charter requirement: "The title Observer-Rooted Asymmetry must be understandable from the paper itself" — the reader should be able to say what "observer-rooted" means, what asymmetry is rooted in, what is intrinsic, what is observer-relative, and what remains invariant across readout.

## Context examined
Mechanical search: "observer-rooted" — 1 occurrence (title); "asymmetr*" — 5 occurrences total (title, keywords, §2.5 forward fence, §8.3 twice). Full reading of §8.3, §10.2, §12 preamble/§12.1, abstract, conclusion. Cards: ROLEDEF_008_014 (reference role), THM_010_013, THM_016_001 — none assembles the phrase either (nor should they; it is the paper's job).

## Strongest favorable interpretation
The components are all present and correct: §8.3 defines readout asymmetry as orientation selection by the reference-role occupant; §10.2 designates the observer cell; the §12 construction is "rooted" at that record; the abstract's second paragraph paraphrases the whole idea. A careful reader can assemble the title.

## Why that interpretation is insufficient
The charter's test is not "can a maximally attentive reader assemble it" but "is it understandable from the paper" — and the assembly requires connecting a §8 definition, a §10 designation, and a §12 adjective across forty pages with no signpost. Reviewers and readers routinely judge a foundational paper partly by whether its title cashes out; this one cashes out only implicitly. The cost is pure opportunity: the paper owns a good, precise answer and never says it.

## Reader consequence
"What does the title actually mean?" remains an exercise; in the worst case a referee answers it wrongly (e.g., reading "asymmetry" as CP-like or the observer as generative).

## Smallest sufficient repair
One short paragraph, placed either in the (recommended) Introduction or at the end of §8.3, of roughly this content (author's phrasing to prevail): *"The title can now be stated exactly. The asymmetry of this paper is readout asymmetry: the orientation selection made when one relational standpoint occupies the reference role. It is rooted in the observer in the sense that the designated reference cell is the root of the readout — and of the rooted one-cell record from which the spectral construction begins. What is intrinsic is the unordered three-amplitude spectrum and its exact ratios; what is observer-relative is the ordered presentation, the selected reading side, and the reporting scale; what is invariant across readout is the unordered spectrum, both ratios, the counts 206 and 409, the screen factor, and the Koide shape."* (If placed in §8.3, the last clause about 206/409 can be deferred with a forward pointer.)
