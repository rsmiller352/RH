# Λ_FCC := span_ℤ{(0,1,1),(1,0,1),(1,1,0)} — the even-coordinate-sum sublattice of ℤ³

## 0) Governance & Scope Lock

the lattice physical, forced, or optimal. Layer ceiling S03.

## 1) Plain language

The section's lattice is the set of integer triples whose coordinates sum to an even
number — equivalently, everything you can build from the three vectors (0,1,1), (1,0,1),
(1,1,0) with integer counts. This page defines that object and proves the two
descriptions agree. Why THIS lattice matters is later business: under the section's
declared hypothesis it is the only lattice that can carry the cell (a theorem, proved on
its own page) — it is never "because it packs well."

## 2) Statement

$$\Lambda_{\mathrm{FCC}} := \mathrm{span}_{\mathbb Z}\{(0,1,1),\ (1,0,1),\ (1,1,0)\}
\;=\; \{(x,y,z) \in \mathbb{Z}^3 : x+y+z \ \text{even}\}$$

— a rank-3 sublattice of $\mathbb{Z}^3$ of index 2; covolume
$\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&0&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix}\right| = 2$.

## 4) Justification (DEF obligation: well-formedness — the two descriptions agree)

**(span ⊆ even-sum).** Each generator has coordinate sum 2; coordinate sums are
$\mathbb{Z}$-linear, so every $\mathbb{Z}$-combination has even sum.

**(even-sum ⊆ span).** Let $(x,y,z)$ have even sum and seek integers $a, b, c$ with
$a(0,1,1) + b(1,0,1) + c(1,1,0) = (b+c,\ a+c,\ a+b) = (x,y,z)$. Solving:

$$a = \frac{-x+y+z}{2}, \qquad b = \frac{x-y+z}{2}, \qquad c = \frac{x+y-z}{2}.$$

Each numerator differs from $x+y+z$ by an even number ($2x$, $2y$, $2z$ respectively),
so all three are even exactly when $x+y+z$ is even — the witnesses are integers, and the
substitution check confirms they solve the system. Hence equality.

**(rank, index, covolume).** The determinant of the generator matrix is
$0\,(0-1) - 1\,(0-1) + 1\,(1-0) = 2 \neq 0$: rank 3, covolume 2; index
$[\mathbb{Z}^3 : \Lambda_{\mathrm{FCC}}] = 2$ (the parity of the coordinate sum is a
surjection onto $\mathbb{Z}/2$ with kernel $\Lambda_{\mathrm{FCC}}$ — stated as the
standard index reading of the covolume; the even/odd coset split is the value-level
content).
