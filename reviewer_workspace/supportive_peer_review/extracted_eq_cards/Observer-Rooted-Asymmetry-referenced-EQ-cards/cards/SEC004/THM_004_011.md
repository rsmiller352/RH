# Packing density, no Kepler input — D = Vol(B(r))/Vol(Cell(r)) = (4/3)πr³ / (4√2 r³) = π/(3√2), in finite exact steps

## 0) Governance & Scope Lock

## 1) Plain language

Put the biggest ball that fits inside each cell — it touches all twelve faces, and
neighboring balls just touch each other. What fraction of each cell does its ball fill?
The ball's volume over the cell's volume: (4/3)πr³ over 4√2·r³. The size r cancels
completely, leaving the pure number π/(3√2). No claim is made that this is the best
possible arrangement of balls in space — that famous question is simply never asked
here. This is the exact bookkeeping share of the filled part of OUR cell.

## 2) Statement

Let $r$ be the cell inradius (THM_004_010) and $B(r)$ the inradius ball at the cell
center. Then:

1. **(Inscription and tangency.)** $B(r) \subseteq \mathrm{Cell}(r)$, touching all 12
   facet planes; neighboring balls (at face-neighbor sites) are tangent and the family
   over all sites is pairwise non-overlapping.
2. **(The exact share.)**
$$D \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{Vol}(B(r))}{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathrm{Cell}(r))}
\;=\; \frac{\tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3}{4\sqrt2\, r^3} \;=\; \frac{\pi}{3\sqrt2},$$
scale-free (every $r > 0$ gives the same value), derived in finitely many exact steps.
**No Kepler/optimality input anywhere.** Discharges F-020/F-035 (the constant now has
an in-spine derivation superseding SPINE_3's undeclared import).

## 4) Proof (complete)

**Clause 1.** The center-to-facet-plane distance is exactly $r$ on all 12 facets, and
no other bounding plane exists (LEM_004_008 via THM_004_010 clause 3): the open ball of
radius $r$ satisfies every facet inequality strictly except at the 12 tangency points —
$B(r) \subseteq \mathrm{Cell}(r)$ touching each facet. Neighboring sites differ by a
minimal vector (length $d = 2r$, THM_004_010 Lemma B): their balls' centers are $2r$
apart — tangent; any two distinct sites are $\ge 2r$ apart (minimal distance), so no
two balls overlap. ∎

**Clause 2.** $\mathrm{Vol}(B(r)) = \tfrac43 \pi r^3$ — the classical ball volume,
recomputed in the exact lane (disc slices: $\int_{-r}^{r} \pi (r^2 - t^2)\,dt =
\pi(2r^3 - \tfrac{2r^3}{3}) = \tfrac43 \pi r^3$; the witness re-derives this
symbolically). Hence

$$D = \frac{\tfrac43 \pi r^3}{4\sqrt2\, r^3} = \frac{4\pi}{3 \cdot 4\sqrt2}
= \frac{\pi}{3\sqrt2},$$

the $r^3$ cancelling exactly (scale-freeness). **Second route** (independent
denominator): THM_004_007's direct $16u^3$ with $r = \sqrt2 u$ gives denominator
$16 (r/\sqrt2)^3 = 4\sqrt2 r^3$ — same quotient. **Nonzero/normalization check:**
$\pi > 0$, $\sqrt2 > 0$ ⟹ $0 < D$, and $D < 1$ ⟸ $\pi < 3\sqrt2$ (proved at
DEF_004_012's bounds clause — consumed there, not asserted here). ∎
