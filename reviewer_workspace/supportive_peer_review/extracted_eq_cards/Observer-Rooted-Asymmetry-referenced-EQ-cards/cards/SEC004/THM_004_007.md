# The explicit cell — C_u has exactly 12 congruent rhombic facets (normals ∝ S_12), 14 vertices (8 deg-3 + 6 deg-4), 6 antipodal facet pairs, volume 16u³

## 0) Governance & Scope Lock

Theorem card with a complete proof. Exact arithmetic only (integer/rational multiples of
the scale u; no decimals). All magnitudes are model-internal (DEF_004_001). The vertex
degree split carries ZERO ontological reading (D-023.7). The name "rhombic dodecahedron"
(RD) is the conventional mathematical name of the proved shape; it imports nothing.
Layer ceiling S03.

## 1) Plain language

Take the twelve halfway planes toward the twelve nearest diagonal directions: together
they carve out a single solid. This page computes that solid completely — every corner,
every face, exactly: 12 identical rhombus faces, 14 corners of two kinds (8 where three
faces meet, 6 where four meet), and volume 16 cubed-units. Nothing about the shape is
assumed — the census is solved, case by case, from the inequalities themselves.

## 2) Statement

Fix $u > 0$ and define, in the licensed presentation,

$$C_u := \bigcap_{n \in S_{12}} \{\, p \in \mathbb{R}^3 : n \cdot p \le 2u \,\}
= \{\, (x,y,z) : |x \pm y| \le 2u,\ |x \pm z| \le 2u,\ |y \pm z| \le 2u \,\}.$$

Then $C_u$ is a compact convex polytope with:

1. **Facets:** exactly 12, one per constraint (every constraint is facet-defining), with
   outward normals proportional to the elements of $S_{12}$; all 12 facets congruent
   rhombi (diagonal half-lengths $\sqrt2\,u$ and $u$);
2. **Vertices:** exactly 14 — $V_3 = \{(\pm u, \pm u, \pm u)\}$ (8 vertices, each on
   exactly 3 facets) and $V_4 = \{(\pm 2u, 0, 0), (0, \pm 2u, 0), (0, 0, \pm 2u)\}$
   (6 vertices, each on exactly 4 facets); the split $14 = 8 + 6$ is combinatorics with
   no ontological reading;
3. **Antipodal pairing:** the facets partition into 6 antipodal pairs
   ($-F_n = F_{-n}$);
4. **Volume:** $\mathrm{Vol}(C_u) = 16u^3$ (exact; two routes agree — facet-pyramid
   decomposition and cube-plus-pyramids).

(The conventional name of this shape is the rhombic dodecahedron, RD.)

## 4) Proof (complete)

**Step 0 (equivalence of the two displays).** Each $n \in S_{12}$ is a signed pair
pattern: $n \cdot p$ ranges over $\pm x \pm y, \pm x \pm z, \pm y \pm z$. The 12
inequalities $n \cdot p \le 2u$ pair into the 6 two-sided bounds $|x \pm y| \le 2u$ etc.

**Step 1 (compactness, convexity, symmetry).** $C_u$ is an intersection of closed
half-spaces: convex and closed. Bounded: $|x+y| \le 2u$ and $|x-y| \le 2u$ give
$|x|, |y| \le 2u$ (average of the two bounds); with the $z$-pairs likewise $|z| \le 2u$.
The group $O_h$ (DEF_004_001) permutes $S_{12}$ (LEM_004_005's shell is
sign-permutation closed) and fixes the support value $2u$, so $O_h C_u = C_u$.

**Step 2 (vertex enumeration — complete case analysis).** A vertex is a point of $C_u$
where the tight constraints' normals span $\mathbb{R}^3$. By the $O_h$-symmetry (Step 1)
it suffices to classify vertices with $x \ge y \ge z \ge 0$ and map back by signed
permutations. So let $p = (x, y, z) \in C_u$, $x \ge y \ge z \ge 0$, and examine which
constraints can be tight ($n \cdot p = 2u > 0$): only those with $n \cdot p > 0$, i.e.
(using the ordering) the candidates are $x + y$, $x + z$, $y + z$, $x - y$, $x - z$,
$y - z$ (each $\le 2u$).

- **Case A ($y = z = 0$, the axis case — the F-105 ellipsis closed).** Candidates reduce
  to $x = x \pm y = x \pm z$ and $y \pm z = 0 \neq 2u$. If $x < 2u$: NO constraint is
  tight — interior-type point, not a vertex (this disposes of every $u < x < 2u$ point
  on the axis). If $x = 2u$: the four constraints $(1,\pm1,0)\cdot p$ and
  $(1,0,\pm1)\cdot p$ are tight; their normals span $\mathbb{R}^3$ (e.g.
  $(1,1,0), (1,-1,0), (1,0,1)$ are independent). Vertex: $(2u, 0, 0)$, on exactly 4
  facets.
- **Case B ($z = 0 < y$).** Tightness of $x - y = 2u$ together with $x + y \le 2u$
  forces $y \le 0$ — excluded. Tightness of $y \pm z = y = 2u$ forces $x \ge 2u$, so
  $x + y \ge 4u$ — excluded. $x \pm z = x = 2u$ forces $x + y > 2u$ — excluded. The only
  possibly-tight constraint is $x + y = 2u$: at most ONE tight normal — not a vertex.
  (No vertex exists in this case; together with Case A this closes the boundary cases
  the corpus proof elided.)
- **Case C ($z > 0$).** $x - y = 2u$ with $x + y \le 2u$ forces $y \le 0$ — excluded;
  likewise $x - z$, $y - z$ tight are excluded. So tight constraints lie among
  $\{x+y,\ x+z,\ y+z\} = 2u$. Three tight: $x = y = z = u$ — the point $(u,u,u)$, normals
  $(1,1,0), (1,0,1), (0,1,1)$ independent — a vertex on exactly 3 facets (every other
  constraint evaluates to $u$ or $0 < 2u$). Two or fewer tight: normals span at most a
  2-plane — not a vertex.

Mapping back under $O_h$: the vertex set is exactly
$V_4 = O_h\,(2u,0,0)$ — 6 points (orbit of an axis point) — and
$V_3 = O_h\,(u,u,u)$ — 8 points. $|V| = 14$, degrees 4 and 3 as computed. ∎(2)

**Step 3 (facets and their shape).** Fix $n = (1,1,0)$; the face
$F_n = C_u \cap \{x + y = 2u\}$. The vertices of $C_u$ lying on this plane are exactly
$(u, u, \pm u)$, $(2u, 0, 0)$, $(0, 2u, 0)$ (inspection of the 14). A face of a polytope
is the convex hull of the polytope's vertices it contains, so $F_n$ is the quadrilateral
with diagonals $(2u,0,0)\!-\!(0,2u,0)$ and $(u,u,u)\!-\!(u,u,-u)$: both have midpoint
$(u, u, 0)$, they are perpendicular ($(-2u, 2u, 0) \cdot (0, 0, -2u) = 0$), with
half-lengths $\sqrt2\,u$ and $u$ — a rhombus (perpendicular bisecting diagonals), of
dimension 2, hence a FACET. By transitivity of $O_h$ on $S_{12}$ (LEM_004_006's orbit
fact is not needed — the direct sign-permutation images suffice), every constraint's
face is a congruent rhombus: 12 facets, every constraint facet-defining. ∎(1)

**Step 4 (antipodal pairing).** $-C_u = C_u$ (if $n \cdot p \le 2u$ for all
$n \in S_{12}$ then, since $-S_{12} = S_{12}$, also $n \cdot (-p) \le 2u$). The
involution $p \mapsto -p$ maps $F_n$ to $F_{-n}$, and $S_{12}$ splits into 6 antipodal
normal pairs (LEM_004_005's shell: each $v$ pairs with $-v$): 6 facet pairs. ∎(3)

**Step 5 (volume, two routes).**
*Route 1 (facet pyramids).* The distance from the origin to each facet plane
$n \cdot p = 2u$ is $2u / \|n\| = 2u/\sqrt2 = \sqrt2\,u$. The rhombus area is half the
product of the diagonals: $\tfrac12 (2\sqrt2\,u)(2u) = 2\sqrt2\,u^2$. Coning each facet
to the origin: $\mathrm{Vol} = \tfrac13 \cdot 12 \cdot (\sqrt2\,u)(2\sqrt2\,u^2)
= \tfrac13 \cdot 12 \cdot 4u^3 = 16u^3$.
*Route 2 (cube plus pyramids).* $C_u = [-u, u]^3 \cup (\text{6 pyramids})$, the pyramid
over each cube face with apex the adjacent $V_4$ vertex (apex height $u$ over a
$2u \times 2u$ base): $(2u)^3 + 6 \cdot \tfrac13 (2u)^2 u = 8u^3 + 8u^3 = 16u^3$. (That
this union IS $C_u$: both are the convex hull of $V_3 \cup V_4$ — the hull of the 14
vertices is $C_u$ since a compact polytope is the hull of its vertices, and the union is
exactly that hull by direct verification on the half-space description: a point of the
hull lies in the cube or in the pyramid of the axis whose coordinate magnitude exceeds
$u$ — at most one coordinate can exceed $u$, since two coordinates $> u$ would violate
their pair constraint.) Routes agree: $16u^3$. ∎(4)
