# The Persistence Corollary — corollary of AX_001_001 (the opposite side of the persistence coin from the Relational Lift LEM_002_008): existence PERSISTS. An admitted relation-token, once admitted, cannot vanish by no-entry; it persists as an accounting obligation until a LICENSED relation (transfer/discharge/closure) closes it. Non-erasure FOLLOWS — it is persistence stated as a prohibition, NOT a separate law and NOT derived from zero-exclusion.

## 1) Plain language

To exist, in this book, is to be a distinguishable relation-token — to stand in a distinction that
the universe of discourse has admitted (AX_001_001). The very first such admission counts as **one**
(THM_002_007). This card asks the second question existence forces, the companion to "what does it
take to stay distinguishable?" (the relational lift, LEM_002_008): **once a thing is admitted and
counted, can it simply disappear?**

The answer is no, and the reason is short. Disappearing — "vanishing by no-entry" — would mean that
a thing which was admitted (had a record) ends up with **no record**: not present in the book at the
far end of the interval. But being admitted is a **relational** fact (AX_001_001 — existence is
relational distinguishability); a thing's admission-status is held by its relations. For that status
to **change** — for an admitted token to become non-admitted — some **relation** must act on it. A
spontaneous vanishing, with no relation doing anything, would be an admitted thing changing its
relational status with **no relational cause** — which is not something the book allows.

And the only **licensed** relations that *can* act on a token are the ones that **move and account
for** it: a transfer (it goes somewhere — "say where it went"), a discharge, a closure. Every one of
these **preserves** the unit — conservation says quantity is moved, never created or destroyed
(LEM_002_005), and the licensed operation set creates no unpaired record (THM_002_012). **None of
them annihilates.** So there is no licensed way to erase an admitted unit; there is only **closing**
it by a licensed relation that accounts for where it went.

Put the two together: an admitted, counted unit cannot vanish by no-entry (no relational cause), and
no licensed operation destroys it (they all preserve). **Therefore it persists** — it stays on the
books as an **accounting obligation** — until a licensed relation **closes** it (posts it, transfers
it, discharges it). That is **non-erasure**: a real completed thing cannot silently go unrecorded.

One careful distinction, the one this card fences: **"no-entry" is not erasure.** No-entry is the
book's way of saying *no admitted unit is present* — an absence, a typed predicate (this is the
zero-exclusion guard, IMPORT_001_007: zero is not an event). It is **not** an operation that *removes*
a present unit. So "I see no record here" never means "a unit was erased here"; it means "no admitted
unit is here." Non-erasure is exactly the rule that you can never get from *a present admitted unit*
to *no record* without a licensed relation in between. **And — important — non-erasure is NOT proved
from zero-exclusion** (the two are different claims: zero-exclusion is about *values*, non-erasure is
about *completed events*); zero-exclusion only keeps the no-entry reading honest. The proof of
non-erasure is the relational-status + conservation argument above.

So: **existence relates (LEM_002_008) and existence persists (this card)** — two corollaries of the
one Law of Existence, and the persistence one, on its own, is everything non-erasure needs.

## 4) Proof (complete — the persistence/non-erasure derivation)

The derivation is the six-step structure RSM ruled (2026-06-13), framed as **conservation of
admitted-account status under licensed operations** — NOT as identity-maintenance of one unit across a
temporal interval (that is LEM_002_008's lane; see §9(ii)/(iii)). The "interval" below is the span over
which **operations** may act on the book; the question is what those operations can do to an admitted
unit, never "is it the same unit later."

**Step 0 (setup — an admitted counted unit; the conservation question).** Let r be a relation-token
admitted as one counted unit of existence: r is admitted (AX_001_001) with existence count
I(r) = 1 (THM_002_007). The question is the **conservation** question: can an admitted counted
unit be removed from the books — pass from **present/admitted** to **no-record** — by anything OTHER
than a licensed relation acting on it?

**Step 1 (admission-status is relationally held).** By AX_001_001 existence is **relational**
distinguishability: r is admitted ONLY as a distinguishable relation-token. So r's admission-**status**
(admitted vs the non-admission boundary) is **held by its relations** — it is a relational fact, not an
intrinsic one.

**Step 2 (a change in admission-status therefore requires a relation).** Because the status is
relationally held (Step 1), a **change** of that status — from admitted to non-admitted — is itself a
**relational act**: some relation must carry it. A relationally-held fact does not change with **no
relation acting**; there is nothing to realize the change. (No status changes itself — the axiom read
at the persistence layer.)

**Step 3 (the operating relations are LICENSED and balance-closed — scoped to the layer, STABLE UNDER
EXTENSION).** What relations *can* act on an admitted unit? The **licensed** ones: the transfer family
(DEF_002_004) and what it generates — the operation set 𝒪 of THM_002_012, which is **balance-closed as
of this wave/layer**. THM_002_012 does NOT claim 𝒪 is absolutely closed for all time (it scopes its
claim "as of this wave" and arms the D-042 missing-operation protocol); so the closure step is stated
with its universe (TD-018) and shown **stable under operation-set extension**: a *future* operation may
become **licensed** ONLY by entering the D-042 protocol, where it must prove its own **balance clause**
— hence any future-licensed operation is **itself balance-closed**, therefore preserving (Step 4). And
a status-changer that is NOT so licensed (outside every balance-clause protocol) is exactly the **D-042
suspect** — diagnosed wrong / missing-operation / reversion-drift (TD-025), never a silent erasure that
defeats the law (it is precisely what falsifier F-NE1 exhibits). **Therefore every operation that is, or
ever becomes, licensed to act on an admitted unit is balance-closed and preserving; an unlicensed
"vanish" is the diagnosed suspect, not a counterexample.** The conclusion (Step 6) holds for ANY
balance-closed 𝒪 — the current one or any D-042 extension — so it does not rest on an un-grounded
"nothing else can ever act" universal.

**Step 4 (licensed relations preserve / account / close — they never annihilate into no-entry).** By
LEM_002_005 transfers **move** quantity, **never create or destroy** it; by THM_002_012 the operation
set is **balance-closed** — no licensed operation creates an **unpaired** record. So every licensed act
on r **moves it** (a transfer: "say where it went"), **discharges** it, or **closes** it — in each case
leaving a paired account of **where the unit went** — and **none annihilates** r into no-record. A
licensed operation that DROPPED r without a paired account would create an unpaired absence, contra
THM_002_012 — which is exactly why no such operation is licensed (F-NE2).

**Step 5 (no-entry is absence, NOT an operation — the guard, not the engine).** Could r "vanish by
no-entry" — could **no-entry itself** remove r? No: **no-entry is not an operation.** No-entry is the
typed **absence** predicate — *no admitted unit is present* (AX_001_001's non-admission boundary). It is
**not a zero value and not a zero event** (the IMPORT_001_007 zero-exclusion guard); it does not **act**
on a present unit, it **describes** the state where no admitted unit is there. So "vanished by no-entry"
can never mean "no-entry erased r"; it could only mean "r's status changed to non-admitted" — which by
Steps 2–4 happens only through a preserving licensed relation. **(Zero-exclusion is used here ONLY as
the guard that absence ≠ event — RSM A-3 — NEVER as the proof of non-erasure.)**

**Step 6 (∴ persistence / non-erasure).** Combine: a status-change requires a relation (Step 2); in the
spine the only such relations are licensed (Step 3); licensed relations preserve/account/close, never
annihilate (Step 4); and no-entry is not an operation that removes a present unit (Step 5). Therefore
**an admitted counted unit cannot pass from present/admitted to no-record except through a licensed
relation that transfers, discharges, or closes it.**

- **Positive face — PERSISTENCE:** an admitted completed unit **remains accountable** — it stays on the
  books as an **accounting obligation** until a licensed relation closes it.
- **Negative face — NON-ERASURE:** it **cannot silently vanish into no-entry** — there is no transition
  from a present admitted unit to no-record without a licensed relation between them.

$$\underbrace{\text{status-change} \Rightarrow \text{a relation}}_{\text{Steps 1–2, AX\_S01\_001}} \ \wedge\ \underbrace{\text{only LICENSED relations act}}_{\text{Step 3, DEF\_S01\_007 / THM\_S01\_016}} \ \wedge\ \underbrace{\text{licensed acts preserve}}_{\text{Step 4, LEM\_S01\_008 / THM\_S01\_016}} \ \Longrightarrow\ \underbrace{\text{remains accountable; cannot vanish into no-entry}}_{\text{Step 6}}.$$

∎ **(the persistence corollary; non-erasure DERIVED as persistence's negative face — NOT posited, NOT a
separate law, and NOT from zero-exclusion.)**

**Why this is DERIVED, not posited, and why NOT from zero-exclusion (the RSM A-3 fence).** The
conclusion is the conjunction of (1) the axiom read at the persistence layer — admission-status is
relationally held, so its change requires a relation (Steps 1–2, AX_001_001, RSM-ruled the legitimate
reading); (2) the operation-set closure — only licensed relations act on admitted units (Step 3,
DEF_002_004 / THM_002_012); and (3) conservation — licensed acts preserve, never annihilate (Step 4,
LEM_002_005 / THM_002_012). Zero-exclusion (Step 5) is a **guard**, not the engine: it only keeps
"vanish by no-entry" from being misread as an erasure event; the **force** comes from (1)+(2)+(3).
Deriving non-erasure from zero-exclusion alone would be the error RSM A-3 names — zero-exclusion is
about *values*, non-erasure about *completed events* — and this proof does not do that. The claim can
lose: F-NE1 (a spontaneous vanish — an unlicensed status-change), F-NE2 (a destroying licensed op),
F-NE3 (no-entry-as-erasure) are executable constructions.
