## 1) Plain language

The next step in the ladder is to *price* the event — to feed it to the substrate's "service/completion cost" operator, `cost[·]`, so a magnitude can eventually fall out. But there's a wall in the way, and it's a deliberate one. The cost operator (`DEF_007_009`) was built to be **pre-readout**: it prices *completed closures* deep in the substrate, and it is explicitly forbidden from touching readout/observer structure (the S07 layer). And `γ_Ψ` is **exactly** a readout/observer thing — the *observer-routed* Now circulation. So "`cost[γ_Ψ]`" asks the pre-readout operator to swallow a readout object. It can't.

The fix is to notice that `γ_Ψ` has **two faces**, and only one of them is the kind of thing cost prices:

- The **posting face** — the two completions (the twins). These are *completed closures*: by the tick↔closure bijection (`THM_007_007`), each forced posting **is** one completed 2π closure. Completed closures are precisely what `cost[·]` is for. So this face **can** be priced — later, by `cost[γ_Ψ^post]`.
- The **routing face** — the single edge the reading travels through. That's *readout* structure (the observer's route), the very thing cost is walled off from. So this face stays **unpriced** — it is carried forward as readout, not as a cost.

This card just draws that line: posting side → priceable; routing side → readout, unpriced. It **prices nothing yet** (that's the cost step, later), it **combines nothing** (the two counts stay apart — this is a separation, not a ratio), and it **says nothing about Ψ's value** (still open). It makes the *next* step legal: we will write `cost[γ_Ψ^post]`, never `cost[γ_Ψ]`.
