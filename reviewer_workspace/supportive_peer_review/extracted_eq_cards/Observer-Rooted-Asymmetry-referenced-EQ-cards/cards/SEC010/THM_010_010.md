## 1) Plain language

The first two four-cell readings gave time a **now** (simultaneity) and an **order** (persistence — A deeper than B than the twins). This third reading asks two questions people usually run together — *what tells the twins apart?* and *which way does time point?* — and shows they have **different answers**.

Look at the twins **C** and **D**: born together, run equally far, so by every **content** measure they read the **same**. The only thing that tells them apart is **σ** — *which one the observer takes as its reference*. Swap the observer (the other chirality) and the reference flips: which twin is "the reference one" is **observer-relative**. That is **relative distinguishability** — and it is a fact about the **reference frame** (which cell is "here"), a *spatial* orientation, not about time's direction.

Now the arrow. *Which way is forward?* That is fixed by something the observer can't touch: **records don't vanish** (non-erasure), and every escapement **adds exactly one** (ΔI = +1). So there are only ever **more** records as you go, never fewer — "more records = later" is just a fact about the count. Swapping the observer (ι_or) re-labels which end of a transfer is the source; it **cannot change how many records there are**. So every observer agrees on the direction: **the arrow of time is invariant** — objective, the same forward for everyone.

The earlier version of this card made one mistake: it called the arrow **σ** and concluded the arrow was observer-relative. But σ is the *reference orientation* (a spatial which-end fact), and the arrow is the *direction of accumulation* (a temporal how-many fact). ι_or swaps the first and leaves the second alone. So: **σ is the observer's frame (varies); the arrow is non-erasure-fixed (invariant); ι_or swaps the frame, not the direction.** (And this is still the **internal** arrow — the physical/thermodynamic arrow you feel is a later, forward job.)
