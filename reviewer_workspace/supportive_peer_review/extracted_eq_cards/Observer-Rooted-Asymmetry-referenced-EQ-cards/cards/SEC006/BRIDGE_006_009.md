## 1) Plain language

The floor card said: if two histories are closer than one resolution unit in action, you cannot tell
them apart — nothing is resolved (the bottom edge of the window). This card builds the **top edge**,
and it is a different kind of statement: it lives on the **bridge** to ordinary physics. Internally,
two histories very far apart in action (a huge gap) is nothing special — it is just a large count.
But the moment you read the framework the way ordinary quantum mechanics does — as an interference of
e^{iS/ħ} contributions — something happens at large action gaps: the phase spins so fast that
neighboring contributions cancel each other out (destructive interference), and the net coherent
contribution is **washed out**. So in the *physical reading*, very-large-gap histories fall **outside**
the coherent window. That is the ceiling.

Two honesties matter, and RSM made both sharp. First, "large gap" alone is **not enough**: the
cancellation only happens if the phase is genuinely *oscillating fast everywhere* — if there is a
**stationary point** (a place the phase momentarily stops changing), that spot survives and dominates,
and there is **no** suppression. So the no-stationary-point condition is **part of the claim**, and a
stationary phase is a way the claim can **fail**, not a fine-print caveat. Second, we connect the
internal unit u_Φ to the physical ħ only as a **declared translation for this one card** — we are
*not* claiming we have built the full bridge or that u_Φ *is* ħ; ħ stays a bridge-side physical thing,
u_Φ stays our internal unit. And the cancellation math itself (the method of stationary phase) is a
**standard result we import and have checked**, not something we re-prove here. This card establishes
only the top edge; stitching the floor and ceiling into the single aperture object A_Φ is the next
card's job.
