## 1) Plain language

We built the bottom edge of the coherence window (the floor: closer than one resolution unit in
action ⟹ nothing is resolved) and the top edge (the ceiling: a very large action gap washes out
under the physical e^{iS/ħ} reading). This card makes the **single object** they bound — the
**coherence aperture** A_Φ — and it does exactly one thing: it **says what kind of object A_Φ is**.
It does **not** yet prove the edges fit together cleanly (that the three regimes are disjoint and
cover the range) — that proof is the next card (THM_006_012). Here we only **type** it.

A_Φ is an **honest two-faced object**. Its **inside face** is entirely ours: the resolved regime,
written only in internal terms (the action-gap g_Φ, the unit u_Φ, the count I), bounded below by the
floor. This face is load-bearing — it stands on the finished floor spine alone, with no physics in
it. Its **outside face** is the top edge, and that edge lives on the **bridge**: it is the
suppression boundary from BRIDGE_006_009, and we **borrow it as a conditional clause** — "under the
declared u_Φ↔ħ translation and the imported cancellation estimate, the top edge is the far-supra-unit
regime." We do **not** copy the physics into the inside face; we **point at** the bridge card that
owns it. The fence is the whole game: u_Φ stays inside, ħ stays on the bridge, and the two faces are
kept **separately typed** so nobody can later quietly fold the physics into the internal definition.

And the **middle** — the band between "just resolved" and "washed out" — we leave **honestly open**.
We do **not** invent a sharp number where coherence ends; that sharp value would need the bridge
actually built (later work). The middle is simply "the resolved window between the two boundaries."
