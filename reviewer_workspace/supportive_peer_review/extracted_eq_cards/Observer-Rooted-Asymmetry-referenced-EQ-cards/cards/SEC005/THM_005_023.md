## 1) Plain language

*(The ≈ decimals in this section are illustration only — P8; the exact symbolic forms (Ω = 1−π/(3√2), 3Ω = 3−π/√2, the windows 15√2/7, 9√2/4) are in §2/§4.)*

Each cell carries a leftover that rises by Ω (≈ 0.26) each tick; at a whole unit the cell completes one unit, a new cell is born, and the bit past 1 carries over.

Start with one cell, A. After three ticks A's leftover is only ≈ 0.78 — not full; the fourth tick pushes it over, so A's first child B is born at tick 4, and A keeps the small leftover (≈ 0.04). Each now runs on its own books. A must refill from that 0.04 — three more ticks reach only ≈ 0.82 (still short), so it takes four more, and A completes again at **tick 8**. B started fresh at tick 4 and, like any fresh cell, takes four ticks to first fill — also **tick 8**. So at tick 8 **both** complete at once: two cells born together → the count goes straight from 2 to 4.

It never sits at 3: the cell that would be "the third" (A's second) and the "fourth" (B's first) are born in the same instant. The only way a 3 would appear is if A's second completion came a tick earlier (tick 7) — which needs A's 0.04 leftover to cross in three ticks. It can't (0.04 + 3·Ω ≈ 0.82 < 1), and the exact statement of "can't" is π > 15√2/7, which π satisfies. So the jump 2→4 is forced. (Three is still the smallest cell-count you could draw a closed loop around, A→B→C→A; the birth process just doesn't pause there. What four cells then *mean* is a later step.)

## 4) Proof (complete, exact)

Throughout, Ω = 1 − π/(3√2) symbolic; a cell escapes at the cycle when its accumulated leftover first reaches the unit I=1 (`AX_001_001`), creating one cell (`THM_005_020`; event vocabulary `ROLEDEF_005_018`), the excess carried over (`THM_005_015`); each cell on its own book (`THM_005_020`).

**Step 1 (origin's first gap = 4 — cited).** By `THM_005_017`, the origin's first escapement is at **tick 4** (⌊3Ω⌋ posts no tick — 3Ω ∈ (3/4,1), record-absent; ⌊4Ω⌋ = 1), and the carried-over leftover is **ε₄ = 4Ω − 1** (strictly positive, `THM_005_017`/`THM_005_015`). It creates the first child. Population 1→2 at tick 4. ∎(1)

**Step 2 (origin's second gap = 4, forced by π > 15√2/7 — the new content).** From ε₄ the origin accumulates Ω per tick. After 3 more ticks: ε₄ + 3Ω = (4Ω−1) + 3Ω = 7Ω − 1, **below the unit** iff 7Ω − 1 < 1 ⟺ 7Ω < 2 ⟺ Ω < 2/7 ⟺ 1 − π/(3√2) < 2/7 ⟺ π/(3√2) > 5/7 ⟺ **π > 15√2/7** (exact, ×3√2). Since π > 15√2/7 (true), **3 more ticks do not cross**. After 4 more ticks: ε₄ + 4Ω = 8Ω − 1 ≥ 1 ⟺ Ω ≥ 1/4 ⟺ π ≤ 9√2/4 — the upper bound of the {3,4} window, **consumed from `THM_005_017`** (true). So the origin's second escapement is at tick 4 + 4 = **tick 8** (gap₂ = 4). ∎(2)

**Step 3 (first child's first gap = 4).** The first child is born at tick 4 register-absent — the empty-history predicate (`DEF_005_003`/`GUARD_005_008`, TD-026), no leftover, never a residual value 0. From its first tick it accumulates Ω on its own book; its first escapement is the smallest k with ⌊kΩ⌋ = 1 — exactly Step 1's computation — so k = 4: the child's first escapement is at tick 4 + 4 = **tick 8** (child-gap₁ = 4). ∎(3)

**Step 4 (the coincidence ⟹ 2→4, skip 3).** By Steps 2–3 the origin's second escapement and the first child's first escapement both occur at **tick 8**. Two cells are created at the same cycle, all within the one existence (`AX_001_001`; each its own book, `THM_005_020`): the population goes **2 → 4** at tick 8. Over cycles the population is 1 (0–3), 2 (4–7), 4 (8) — **never a stable 3**. Counterfactually, were gap₂ = 3 (which by Step 2 needs π ≤ 15√2/7), the origin's third cell would appear at tick 7 — before the child's — and the count would pass through 3 (2→3 at 7, 3→4 at 8). Since π > 15√2/7, this does not happen: the skip is **forced**. ∎(4)
