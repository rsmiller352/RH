## 1) Plain language

*(This section alone uses the internal observer's temporal idiom — readout language; the
claims and proof are order-theoretic.)*

Suppose the ledger owes another attempt to close — a leftover stands below one whole unit,
and something further must service it. What can that "something further" be, in the running
machinery as actually built? This page answers: exactly one thing — a base tick, the step
that adds the share Ω to the residual register. Nothing else is on the books. A posting
can't do it: postings fire only when a whole unit completes, and the leftover hasn't
completed one (that's the point of servicing it). A second register or hidden coordinate
can't hold it: the core runtime state is one register and nothing more. A do-nothing step
can't count: an event that changes nothing is no event at all in this framework. No other
amount can arrive: the tick's share is typed once, the state has nowhere else to receive,
and records never feed backward into the state they came from. And nothing can slip in or
out unpaired: every licensed move balances. So when the theory elsewhere proves that a
further closure-attempt is forced, this page supplies the identification: at the runtime
layer, that attempt IS one base tick. The escapement — the posting — remains what it always
was: forced by completion, not by the standing leftover. Completion forces the posting;
persistence forces the next attempt; and the next attempt wears exactly one uniform.
