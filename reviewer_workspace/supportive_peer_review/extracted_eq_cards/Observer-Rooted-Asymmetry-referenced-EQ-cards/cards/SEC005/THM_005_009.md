## 1) Plain language

Here is the question this page answers: why does the runtime have to fire a tick at all, and
why exactly one whole unit, when a leftover fills up? Earlier work hoped a symmetry argument
would force it; a careful re-check (F-182) found that argument quietly assumed its own answer,
and a later stopgap simply DECLARED the answer. Neither is good enough — and the theory does
not allow declarations. The real reason is stronger and simpler. The runtime measures every
leftover against one whole unit of existence, the unit the book starts from, I = 1. A leftover
that has not reached one is not yet a completed thing — there is nothing finished to write down.
But the moment the leftover reaches one, a whole unit of existence has actually completed. And
existence cannot simply vanish unrecorded: the Law of Existence says a completed unit must be
accounted for. So the book has no choice — it must close the unit by posting one event-token.
It cannot say "nothing happened," because something did: one unit completed. And it posts
exactly one, because existence is counted in whole completed units and exactly one whole unit
completed. So ΔI = 1 is not declared and not assumed — it FOLLOWS, because exactly one completed
unit-token had to post. Nowhere did we start by saying "the tick must be one"; we started from
existence, the unit, and the rule that completion must be recorded, and the one came out the end.
