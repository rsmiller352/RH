# The completed-unit count and the unitized residual ratio — ⌊u⌋ is the COUNT OF COMPLETED UNIT-ACCOUNTS, frac(u) is the UNITIZED RESIDUAL RATIO relative to the next unit (account completion, NOT real-number floor math — D-049/TD-028); the residual ratio strictly between the floor and the next unit; the R<1/R=1/R>1 account states

## 0) Governance & Scope Lock

## 1) Plain language

Any running account splits cleanly into the whole units already completed and the leftover
still filling toward the next unit. This page names that split — but not as real-number
arithmetic on a free-floating decimal. The whole part is a COUNT: how many whole units of
existence have completed. The leftover is a RATIO: how far the account has filled toward the
next whole unit, measured against the unit that completes (against one — DEF_005_005). A
decimal like 0.3 is not a little piece of existence; it is the unit-relative reading of the
leftover, and if you changed the measuring unit the decimal would move without anything about
existence changing. When there is a leftover, it is a strictly-positive ratio, smaller than one
whole unit — and when there is none, that is not "a leftover of zero," it is simply no held
residue (the register is not in the book). The old work wrote the leftover's range as "from
zero up to one"; this framework drops the zero end — the residue is either strictly positive
and present, or absent as a typed fact.

## 2) Statement

For a running account u (a strictly-positive accumulation on the runtime residual scale,
measured against the unit account I = 1 — the register's content, DEF_005_003; the unit,
DEF_005_005), the **completed-unit-count / residual-ratio decomposition** is

$$\lfloor u \rfloor \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}, \qquad \operatorname{frac}(u) = u - \lfloor u \rfloor, \qquad u = \lfloor u \rfloor + \operatorname{frac}(u),$$

where **⌊u⌋ is the COUNT OF COMPLETED UNIT-ACCOUNTS** (how many whole units of existence have
completed — counted in units of I = 1; the I = 1 value ← THM_002_007 (D-051), displayed via DEF_005_005, P1 the counting principle). **The count's codomain is ⌊u⌋ ∈ ℤ≥0** [precision amendment 2026-06-13, OBS-1]: pre-threshold (u < 1) the count is **⌊u⌋ = 0 — NO completed unit, the record-absent / empty-history predicate, NEVER an internal floor-zero object** (D-050: the floor is the EXTERNAL completed-crossing count, and "no completed unit" is absence, not a zero value); **⌊u⌋ ≥ 1 only once a whole unit has completed (u ≥ 1)**. So "⌊u⌋ ∈ ℤ≥1" applies STRICTLY to the completed case (u ≥ 1), never to pre-threshold u < 1. And **frac(u) is the UNITIZED RESIDUAL RATIO** (the
leftover relative to the next unit, R_acc against I = 1 — DEF_005_005). This is ACCOUNT
COMPLETION, not real-number floor math (D-049/TD-028): the exact arithmetic is identical, but
⌊·⌋ counts completed accounts and frac is the unit-relative ratio, never a free-floating real
decimal. The residual ratio is **strictly between the floor and the next unit** when present:
frac(u) is strictly positive (the residue-present case) and frac(u) < 1 (the threshold
predicate). The old work's "0 ≤ ε < 1" re-types here: the lower bound is dropped — the
**no-residue case is the typed predicate** (no held residue remains; the residual register is
absent at DEF_005_003), NOT the value frac(u) = 0; and the runtime DOMAIN on declared residual
quantities is strictly positive (the "u ≥ 0" is retired — P3/GUARD_S04_016). The three account
states (DEF_005_005) read off the decomposition: frac(u) > 0 with ⌊u⌋ ≥ 0 completed is the R<1
filling state of the leftover; an exact whole-unit completion is the R=1 boundary; ⌊u⌋ ≥ 1 with
a carried strictly-positive frac is the R>1 state.
