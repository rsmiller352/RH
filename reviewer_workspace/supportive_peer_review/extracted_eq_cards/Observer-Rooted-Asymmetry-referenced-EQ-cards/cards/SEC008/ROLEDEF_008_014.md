## 1) Plain language

The earlier cards built the reader **L** and a pile of available, registered states — but said nothing about
*what makes reading possible at all*. This card supplies that one thing: **to read a distinction, you need
something to read it against — a reference.**

Picture yourself as a cell in a two-cell system. You cannot step outside and see the whole; the dimensionality
of what you can tell apart is tiny. But you **can** distinguish — because the two cells' books carry different
amounts of leftover (different residuals), and *you, being one of them, are the reference the other is read
against.* That is the whole of it: **the observer is the reference.** All you can tell is "the other has gone
through more than I have" — a distinction read relative to **you**.

Three things this card is careful about. **The observer is a role, not a thing.** Nothing is *inherently* an
observer — not a cell, not a subsystem, not the whole system; each is observer-like only when it is **read in
the reference role**. **You don't need a fourth cell, or any particular number, to be an observer.** One cell
can occupy the role — it just has nothing to be reference *to* until a second registered state is there; the
distinguishability grows as more states appear. And **the observer does not *create* the distinction.** The
leftover imbalance is already there, primitive, whether or not anyone reads it. What the observer does is
**select the side it reads from** — it *induces the asymmetry of the reading* by being the reference. The
asymmetry **is** the observer; it is not something the observer manufactures out of nothing.

This card stops there. It does **not** put the readings in order, does **not** make a clock, does **not**
measure time. It only forces the reference that every later comparison and clock will need.
