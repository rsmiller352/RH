## 1) Plain language

A bookkeeping correction to the order of work — nothing more.

When S07 opened, its register (GATE_008_001) listed the readout obligations and, for physical time, wrote
the order as: first calibrate (RO-E, the κ step), then define physical time (RO-D). That ordering bundled
"what physical time **is**" together with "what unit we **measure** it in."

Since then we noticed two things already on the books. First, time is **already defined** without any κ:
THM_002_014 (the Theorem of Time) says one closure has the dimensionless extent 2π, and that *is* time — in
closure-readout units, no seconds, no calibration. Second, the contract that governs a physical-time bridge
keeps three things on **separate lines**: the time **variable**, its **units**, and the **calibration κ**.

So RSM re-scoped: **define physical time first, calibrate later.** RO-D becomes the κ-free readout — the
observer-system reading its own completed updates in a clockhood configuration, counted in closure-readout
units. RO-E becomes the *later* job of attaching an external/SI unit, where κ shows up only as bridge-side
shorthand for that calibration relation — never a free-standing constant. The new order is **RO-C → RO-D →
RO-E**.

Three cares. The old register card is promoted and frozen, so we do **not** edit it; this new card records
the re-scope and supersedes **only** the RO-D/RO-E ordering lines. This card **defines neither** RO-D nor
RO-E — it just fixes their order and scope. And until this card is itself promoted, the **old** order stays
in force. It builds no bridge, applies no unit, performs no calibration, and makes no prediction — it keeps
the books straight before the next card.
