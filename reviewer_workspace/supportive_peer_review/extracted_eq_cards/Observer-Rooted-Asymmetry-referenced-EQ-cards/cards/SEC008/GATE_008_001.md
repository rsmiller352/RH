## 1) Plain language

S06 finished a complete, closed job: it showed — *internally, before any physics* — that the substrate's
update accounting and its closure accounting are **one** process, not two (the internal identity), and it
stopped exactly at the edge of physics, recording its own status as *"internally discharged +
bridge-deferred."* Everything past that edge — what an **observer** reads, what a **clock** is, what
**physical time** is, how the internal cost becomes **energy**, what the theory **predicts** — was left
deliberately unbuilt, behind a boundary.

This card is the **doorway** to that next room, and nothing more. It does not furnish the room. It does three
plain things. **First**, it opens the room (S07) and writes down the **list of jobs** that must be done
inside it — define the observer/readout, define what a readout is, define clockhood, define physical time,
fix the calibration, connect to energy, make predictions — and marks **every one of them as still to do**.
**Second**, it writes the **house rules** any of those jobs must follow: take your inputs only from the
completed internal record (never invent a continuous phase or a clock out of thin air); carry the orientation
through; satisfy the imported nine-point time-bridge checklist before claiming any measured rate or clock
time; and — the load-bearing rule — the "observer" means *a relationship through which distinctions get
realized*, **not a mind or a person watching**. **Third**, it fixes the one-way direction: the new room
**reads from** the finished internal structure; it never reaches back and re-writes it. The S06 result stays
exactly as it was.

So this card builds no bridge. It carries no physical time, no energy, no calibration constant, no equation,
no prediction. It is the contract that says *what the bridge will have to be*, written so that the first real
bridge card can be checked against it — the readout-side twin of the contract S06 itself opened with.
