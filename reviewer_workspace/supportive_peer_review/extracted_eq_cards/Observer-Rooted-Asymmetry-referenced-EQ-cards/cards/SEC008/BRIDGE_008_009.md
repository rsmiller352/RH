## 1) Plain language

RO-D gave the reader a **time** reading: each closure is worth 2π of extent, counted up. RO-Dm gave the reader
the **other side** of the same closure: each closure carries 1/(2π) of conjugate **support**, the reciprocal of
its extent. Both are pure, unit-free, "closure-readout" readings — numbers in the book, not yet anything you could
report to a laboratory.

This card is the **doorway to reporting**. It says: to *report* those two readings in any unit, you attach a
**scale** — but the scale is the **observer's** scale (the resolution scale of the reading relationship), not a
fixed laboratory unit. Pick an observer-scale **time unit** for the time reading, and — because the two readings
are reciprocal (`m_base·t_base = I`) — the observer-scale **support unit** is **forced** to be its reciprocal, so
the relationship between them survives. That single coupled choice **is** the bridge. It does both sides at once,
on purpose: bridging time without support would leave the conjugate uncalibrated and break the reciprocal tie.

Three cares. **The target is generic, not SI.** "Seconds and kilograms" are just **one** observer scale — the
**human-instrument** one — and it's a *reporting convention*, not the fundamental thing; physical time and the
support reading exist before anyone reports them in seconds. So this card names SI only as a **fenced special
case** and does **not** hard-code "kilogram" (the support reading is not yet physical mass — that identification
is a later bridge). **It's a convention map, not new physics.** It performs **no** calibration constant (κ here
is shorthand for *the calibration relation, through the clock role*, never a free number), invents no new object,
and changes neither RO-D nor RO-Dm. And **it claims no measured physics.** No measured rate, no Lorentz, no
dilation, no energy, no gravity, no prediction — those wait behind the full contract and the later bridges. This
card stops at: *the declared, coupled, observer-scale way to report the reciprocal pair.*
