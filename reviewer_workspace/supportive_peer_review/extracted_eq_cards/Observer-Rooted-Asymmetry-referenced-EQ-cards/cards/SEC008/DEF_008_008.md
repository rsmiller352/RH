## 1) Plain language

RO-A built the reader L. RO-B said what counts as a registered completed update. RO-C said *when* that material
functions as a clock — it puts the updates in **order**. RO-D supplied **time**: each completed closure is worth
**2π** of *extent*, and time is the reader accumulating that extent over its closures.

This card supplies the **other side** of the same closure. A closure has an extent (that became time) *and* a
**support** — the thing that lets one unit of existence close with **no remainder**. The Theorem of Time
(THM_002_014 §5) already named that support: `m_base = 1/(2π)`, the **conjugate** of the extent, with
`m_base · t_base = I`. So the conjugate closure support, here, is simply: the observer-system **reads each of its
completed closures as carrying that per-closure support** — "each closure carries `1/(2π)` of support, the
reciprocal of its `2π` of extent."

Three cares. **It supports, it doesn't time** — the difference from RO-D is exactly which factor of
`m_base·t_base = I` you read: RO-D reads the extent (and adds it up), RO-Dm reads the support. **It is
per-closure, not a running total** — the support is the per-closure intensity `m_base`; whether you could *add
it up* over many closures is a later, derived question, and deliberately **not** what this card defines (a
running total would just re-count the units; §9(vii)). And **it is unit-free** — it tells you "the reciprocal
of the turn," not "so many kilograms"; putting a unit on it (a calibration constant κ, an SI mass) is **later**,
RO-E, and this card only *points* at that job. It also claims **no energy** (that's RO-F, which takes this
support and distributes it across the channels), **no gravity** (mass's partner here is *time*, not gravity),
and **no prediction** (RO-G). This card stops at the bare κ-free conjugate-support reading.
