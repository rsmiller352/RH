## 1) Plain language

RO-A built the reader L. RO-B said what counts as a registered completed update. RO-C said *when* that material
functions as a clock — it puts the updates in **order**, but on purpose it stopped there: order, not duration.

This card supplies the one remaining thing for **time**: a **size for each step**. The Theorem of Time
(THM_002_014) already told us what one completed closure is *worth* as time — a full turn, **2π** of angular
extent, a dimensionless, unit-free amount. So physical time, here, is simply: the observer-system **reads its
own completed closures** (it already knows how many — that's the count I), and **each one carries 2π of
extent**. Add them up and you have a time reading — *"so many full turns of extent along."*

That is the whole RO-D object. Three things it is careful about. **It times, it doesn't just order** — the
difference from clockhood is exactly the 2π extent per closure; without it you'd have RO-C renamed. **It is
unit-free** — it tells you "how many turns of extent," not "how many seconds"; putting a second on it (a unit,
a calibration constant κ) is a **later** job, RO-E, and this card only *points* at that job, it does not do it.
And **it claims no physics** — no measured rate, no Lorentz, no dilation, no energy, no prediction; those wait
behind the calibration. This card stops at the bare κ-free time reading.
