## 1) Plain language

RO-A built the reader L and an **unordered** pile of available completions 𝖣(L). RO-B said **what counts as
registered** (a yes/no membership). Neither put the pile in order — on purpose. This card supplies the one
remaining thing: **when does that registered material function as a clock?**

The answer is **not** "when it becomes a thing called a clock." Nothing is inherently a clock — not a
wristwatch, not a cesium atom, not a heartbeat, not a sundial. They become clock-like only when **read in a
certain role**. So this card defines a **role**, not an object.

The role has three parts, and it's exactly a sundial. **(1) A succession** — something that advances. Here it's
the completions, which carry an order from the existence count (each completion is one more). That order is the
**shadow that moves**. **(2) A comparison standard** — something to read the advance *against*. Here it's the
closure cycle (one full 2π turn) — the **dial markings**. But the dial is not a background object sitting
behind the world: it works as a clock only because the shadow is **compared against** it (shadow-position
*relative to* marking). What makes a clock is the **comparison relation**, not the dial's stillness — and a
*moving* reference (another recurrence) would do just as well. **(3) The reading** — the locus L reads the
moving succession against the comparison standard and gets an **ordered measure**: "so many turns along." That
reading is the clock.

Three things this card is careful about. **The order is in the reading, not in the pile** — 𝖣(L) stays
unordered; the clock is a way of *reading* it, not a re-arrangement of it. **The reference is a relation, not a
background** — you do *not* need a second moving thing (a single comparison baseline is enough), but neither is
the reference a fixed external grid or absolute frame; it is a relational comparison standard (the framework
has no absolute clock — ROLEDEF_006_008). And **the measure is relative and unit-less** — it tells you "how many
turns," not "how many seconds"; there is no absolute clock and no physical unit. Putting a physical unit on it
(seconds), or reading it as real-time, is a **later** job (physical time); calibrating the unit is later still.
This card stops at the bare readable order.
