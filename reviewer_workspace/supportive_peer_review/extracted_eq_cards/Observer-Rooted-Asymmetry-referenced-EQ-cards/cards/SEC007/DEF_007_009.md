## 1) Plain language

Before the junction can talk about energy, it needs the honest internal thing energy will later be a
reading of: **what it costs, inside the substrate, to complete one unit of distinguishability.** Call that
the **service/completion cost**. Every time a distinction completes a relational closure — one whole 2π
turn, one forced posting, one completed unit — some **servicing** had to happen to carry it to completion.
cost[·] names that servicing burden.

It is a **burden, not a tally.** Counting the completed closures (one per unit) tells you *how many* units
completed; cost[·] tells you *the service it took to complete them* — a strictly-positive amount (one
unit's worth of service per closure), measured in the substrate's own unit. The count indexes the cost;
the cost is the burden.

Three careful limits, all the point of OB-C. **It is pre-physical and it is not energy.** We do not write
the symbol E, we do not call cost "energy," and we use no ħ, no İ = E/ħ, no κ, no physical time — those
all live on the far side of a bridge we are not building (the F-099 fence: cost first, energy later). The
old corpus "service-quantity" (the mass that services one unit per closure) is mentioned only as the prior
*motivation* for the servicing idea, never as an energy equation. **σ rides along.** An oriented closure
carries its σ; cost does not care which orientation it is (the service burden is the same), but cost
**never drops σ** and **never lets you read σ back out of the cost** (or out of the phase). And **cost is
never zero**: if nothing completed, that is "the account is still open," a typed state, not a cost of zero.

This card types that object and stops there. It **sets up** the later energy / physical-time-readout
bridge — but builds none of it, asserts no conjecture, and opens no observer/readout work.
