## 1) Plain language

Think of one business closing its books for a period. The income statement (P&L) takes all the period's
inputs and resolves them into **one** bottom-line result. The balance sheet takes the **prior state** and
carries it forward, updated by the period's change. The closing entry carries the P&L result **into** the
balance sheet (net income → retained earnings). Net income and the change in retained earnings are not two
separate events — they are **two positions in one closing**, the result and the state, tied by the closing
entry.

The substrate's completion works the same way. The **update side** resolves the running residue into **one
forced completed unit** — that is the result, forced by the Law of Existence (THM_S04_006/THM_S04_019). The
**closure/state side** carries the accumulated state and updates it — the books balance, prior plus inputs
equals held plus postings (THM_005_014), and the closure count ticks up by one (ROLEDEF_S05_015/DEF_S06_002).
OB-A is the closing entry: it carries the one forced result **into** that same closure's state update
(THM_007_007). The count is one (OB-B), the service is one (OB-C).

So is this **one** process (one relation, read as result and as state) or **two** processes sharing the
books? For it to be **two**, the second process would need a job of its own — a **second** result, or a
**second** state update, or a **second** service, or some genuinely **new** role, or a distinguishing mark.
But the result is one, the state update is one, the service is one; the two jobs (result, state) are both
already taken and tied together — so any "second process" that tried to claim a **new** job has none to
claim. The one case left is a second process that claims **no** new job at all and just shares the same
books. Picture two alleged companies with the **same** books, same transactions, same closing entries, same
owners and equity, same rights and obligations, same audit trail, same state, and same future consequences.
Accounting has **no basis** for calling them two companies: a bare second "company label" matters only if it
is **admitted somewhere** — as a legal or accounting distinction, a record, a claim, an obligation, a
consequence. A label with no readout, no record, no claim, no consequence is **not** an accounting
distinction. The substrate works the same way: in this book a thing is two only if something **admitted and
readable** tells it apart (AX_001_001 — to exist is to be a distinguishable relation; there is no hidden
back-room ledger where two relations differ while every entry on the books is identical). So the real
question is **not** "is there some hidden difference we haven't found?" — it is "**does the second process
register any admitted, readable difference at all?**" A different count, closure, service cost, orientation,
posting, licensed move, or downstream consequence would do it — and there is **none**. **A second process
that registers no admitted difference is not a second process; it is the same process named twice.** So it is
**one** process, read two ways — the result position and the state position of one closing. (Honest note:
this admission check is doing the real work for the share-everything case — it is not a side cross-check.
Reframed this way it is not a hunt for hidden differences but a check of what is **on the books**: there is no
"off the books" in this framework.)

Two honest limits. The "state" here is the substrate's own **discrete count state** (how many completions
have posted) — **not** physical time, not a clock, not an observer's reading; the books accumulate over the
**cycle index**, before any physics. And this does **not** touch the predecessor identity record — it proves the internal
identity component; the empirical test of the whole picture (the bridge) stays deferred.
