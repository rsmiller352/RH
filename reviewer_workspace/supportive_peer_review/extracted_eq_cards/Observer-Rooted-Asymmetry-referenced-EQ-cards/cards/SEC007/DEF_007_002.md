## 1) Plain language

`ROLEDEF_003_011` said *what* G is: the **bookkeeping closure of the system** — the entry that books whatever the
rest of the system leaves to be booked — and that it reads "nothing left to book" when the system couples only
with itself. That card stopped at the role and left the *value* open. This card takes the next, deliberately
small step: it says *how* G operates, **without putting a number on it**.

The **G-operation** is just this: **go through the system's books and pair off whatever is still unpaired.** The
system's accounting has a slot — category **T3** — for "the remainder a closure leaves open." (We use that slot
only as a *named category*; the *rule* for how much sits there is a separate, still-unwritten theorem, and we do
not touch it.) "Pairing off" is the framework's own balance operation (`THM_002_012`): admissible records come in
oriented pairs; what is left **unpaired** is the residual. So the G-operation is the system-level **reconcile**:
pair the unpaired.

**Complete system closure** — "**G-silence**" — is simply the limit of that: **the state where nothing is left
unpaired.** Then G has nothing to book; its self-coupled reading is the "nothing left" marker (an *absence*
marker, not a count of zero). And — the one thing this card is careful to say twice — **closing the books does
not destroy anything.** Pairing is not deleting: the records are still there, still counted, just *paired*. What
is gone is the unpaired *residue*, not the *existents*.

That is all this card defines: the operation (pair the unpaired) and its limit (nothing left unpaired). It does
**not** say *when or how* the system actually reaches that limit — that is a later theorem (the dynamics). It puts
**no number** on G, defines **no** Ω, and says **nothing** about black holes, area laws, relativity, or measured
gravity. Just: what the G-operation *is*, and what *complete closure* means — value-free.
