card is the bookkeeping checkpoint that writes that down cleanly, so the next stretch starts from an
honest ledger.

discrete thing, and it has a real job — it says which way a closure is oriented, which is the same as
which side of the paired posting a record sits on (that was OB-D2). The path there was: postings come
before any continuous phase (OB-D0), and any future smooth-phase readout has to be built on the bridge
side and carry σ along separately, never read it back out of the phase (OB-D1). Those three — THM_007_003,
GATE_007_004, THM_007_005 — are the discharge. The held diagnostic (THM_S06_003) stays exactly where it
was: named, not used.

What stays open. The big conjecture — that the substrate's update rate equals the phase-closure rate —
still needs three things first: the tick↔closure map (OB-A), the rate equality itself (OB-B), and the
cost operator before any energy (OB-C). One precondition is already handled (the substrate-rate
re-check, folded into the internal-rate card); one piece of transcription is still pending. So the
conjecture stays **last and blocked**, and we lock the order: OB-A, then OB-B, then OB-C, then the
conjecture.

What it refuses to do. It does **not** edit the original frozen contract (it reconciles the ledger in a
fresh card instead). It discharges none of the still-open obligations, asserts none of the conjecture,
and opens none of the closed work (no bridge map, no scheduler, no observer, no S07). And it carries every
standing guard forward unchanged: σ stays discrete, the continuous phase stays a bridge readout, the
squeeze stays a comparison tool, and nothing grows, no ħ leaks in, no zero-object appears.
