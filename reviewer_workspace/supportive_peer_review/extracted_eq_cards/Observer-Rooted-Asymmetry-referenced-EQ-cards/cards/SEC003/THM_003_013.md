## 1) Plain language

Where do the three come from, and where does the nine come from? Both are already in the S01
formalism, before fairness ever speaks.

A closure, in this book, is a **register-walk** (`THM_002_011`): a chained sequence of steps with a
**first step and a start register**. It is *rooted by construction*. So the minimal three-register
closure is not one abstract triangle — it is read from a **root** (a start-register), giving the rooted records `A→B→C→A`, `B→C→A→B`,
`C→A→B→C`. And these are genuinely different formal objects: the only thing the S01 formalism ever
merges is an exact out-and-back retrace (`w·w̄` folds to nothing); *every other walk is alone in its
class*. It installs **no** "rotate the triangle" quotient, **no** "forget the root" quotient. So the
unrooted triangle is not a cheaper object already sitting in the language — it would be a **new,
unlicensed quotient**.

Now the two laws act on these already-admitted records. **Fairness** (`LEM_003_010`) says no position
is singled out by the rules — so you may not keep only the `A`-rooted record (that would distinguish
`A`), nor quotient them away (that would erase distinctions the S01 equivalence does not license). So
the fair account **keeps all the rooted records** (over the three register-roots). **Non-erasure** (`LEM_002_013`) says an admitted
record cannot silently vanish — so the three, once admitted, cannot be quietly collapsed into the
unrooted triangle without a licensed equivalence (there is none). Fairness does not *create* the
three; non-erasure does not *admit* them; both act on what `THM_002_011` already put on the books.

Each rooted record runs through the same three positions. The account is therefore every
`(root, position)` pair: three roots, three positions, **nine**. Not three (that keeps one root or
takes the unlicensed unrooted quotient); not six (unordered erases the root-vs-position roles;
ordered-but-no-diagonal bans `(A,A)` for no reason); **nine** — every rooted-position incidence,
once. That nine is `Cap`.
