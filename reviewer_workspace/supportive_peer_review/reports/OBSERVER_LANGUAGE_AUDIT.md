# Observer-Language Audit

**Question.** Does the manuscript consistently treat the observer as an operational/relational structure rather than a conscious mind, and can a reader explain the title from the paper alone?

**Method.** Every significant occurrence of observer/observation/measurement/readout/viewpoint/perspective/asymmetry/aperture/presentation/distinguishability reviewed in context (mechanical search + reading).

## Findings by term

- **observer.** Uniformly operational. Defined as role (§8.0: "not a psychological word here. It names a relational role"); abstract pre-empts ("The observer does not add consciousness, create orientation, or generate the registered structure. It reads distinctions already carried by the relational record."). §8.3's Obs(X, ℓ) gives it a type signature. No anthropomorphic use found anywhere. **PASS.**
- **observation/measurement.** §1.4 fixes measurement as licensed comparison before any observer exists; §8.2 explicitly separates registration from "measurement in the later physical sense." **PASS.**
- **readout.** Consistent: availability of already-resolved content. The dependency direction ("consumes the completed internal record; does not ground, reopen, or edit it," §8.0) is restated at every layer. **PASS.**
- **viewpoint/perspective.** Essentially absent (no loose perspectival language found — a quiet strength). **PASS.**
- **asymmetry.** Occurs in the body only at §2.5 (forward fence: "Later sections distinguish this ledger balance from observer-induced readout asymmetry") and §8.3 (the definition: orientation selection, "not a signed amount and not an act of creation"). Both occurrences are exact. **But see title finding below.**
- **aperture.** Operationalized fully in §6; bridge faces typed. **PASS.**
- **presentation.** Governed by §§4.1 and 9; consistent. **PASS.**
- **distinguishability.** Primitive; the §8.3 sentence "The observer does not create distinguishability. The distinguishability is primitive and the relevant completion has already been resolved" is the exact anti-idealist sentence the charter asks for. **PASS.**

## Consciousness risk
No passage suggests consciousness creates physical reality; three passages explicitly deny it (abstract, §8.0, §8.3). The paper is about as far from quantum-mysticism language as a paper with "observer" in its title can be. **Risk: NONE at the sentence level.**

## Intrinsic ↔ observer-relative movement
The intrinsic/observer boundary is policed explicitly at every crossing the audit checked (§10's two 2/3s; §12's "no labeled sector is intrinsic"; §13.4's screen typed as readout; §14's naming as downstream convention). One notational blur (A#, I_bulk crossing layers — collision X5/X6) against dozens of correct crossings. The observer nowhere "generates a mass": the supports are intrinsic; the observer's contribution is ordering/presentation selection and the reporting scale. **PASS with the X5/X6 notational footnote.**

## The title finding (principal result of this audit)
**The phrase "observer-rooted" occurs nowhere in the body — only in the title.** "Asymmetry" is explained once (§8.3) without reference to the title. The reader must self-assemble: *the asymmetry* = readout asymmetry (which side is read as reference), *rooted in* = the observer's occupation of the reference role at a designated cell (§10.2's four-cell frame; the "rooted" projector of §12 whose root is that designated cell). All components exist in the paper; the assembly does not. The charter's test — "the reader should be able to explain what 'observer-rooted' means, what asymmetry is rooted in, what is intrinsic, what is observer-relative, and what remains invariant across readout" — currently requires the reader to do the explaining without the paper's help.

**Answer key the paper supports but never states:** the intrinsic content is the unordered three-amplitude spectrum and its ratios; the observer-relative content is the ordered presentation (which D₃ frame), the readout asymmetry (which side is reference), and the reporting scale; invariant across readout are the unordered spectrum, the two ratios, 206, 409, s_409, and the Koide shape. One paragraph saying exactly this — at §8.3, in the (recommended) Introduction, or as the Conclusion's opening — completes the title's contract with the reader.
