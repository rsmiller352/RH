# Section 14 — Charged-Lepton Mass Ratios and the Electron Standard

**Purpose.** Attach the lepton reporting names to the rank triple, introduce the single electron scale, and produce the exact symbolic ratios plus diagnostic decimals.

**Comprehension assessment.** High. The order of operations is stated and honored: "The mathematics fixes the ratios before the external electron record enters." The naming map is honestly typed as a downstream convention over ranks. The bridge ℬ is determined pointwise from ratio preservation plus the single anchor; the rescaling-invariance sentence closes the unit-dependence question. The final displays give the exact symbolic forms first and the decimals second, labeled "diagnostic" — the right hierarchy.

**The constitutive question the section under-serves.** The naming map is typed as convention, and that is correct as far as ordering goes — but the *physical* claim underneath (these three intrinsic quadratic supports are the things an observer reads as charged-lepton masses) is a constitutive identification the paper never states in one sentence. Its ingredients exist: §8.6 (support readout as the mass-side reading), §8.7 (observer-scale reporting), §14.2 (the electron record admitted as the comparison standard *for q_min*). What is missing is the explicit sentence: *identifying the minimum support with the electron record is itself the constitutive step that types all three supports as charged-lepton mass reports; its empirical content is exhausted by the two ratio comparisons.* Without it, a referee can ask "why leptons at all, and why not neutrinos or quark triples?" and find no explicit answer. The honest answer available on the public materials is: the theory derives one distinguished three-rank support spectrum, and the identification with the charged-lepton family is made *at calibration* and validated by the two ratios plus Koide — a modest, defensible claim that should be stated rather than left implicit. (Candidate CI-8; the abstract's "complete relative three-entry report" phrasing already gestures at it.)

**The missing measured comparison (F11).** The section prints the μ and τ outputs to thirty decimals and never quotes a measured muon or tau value. The derived r_μ agrees with CODATA's measured m_μ/m_e = 206.7682827(46) inside the experimental uncertainty; the τ output sits ≈0.6σ from the current measured value. The paper's silence is presumably deliberate (no measured value may enter, even in comparison), but the derivation is *complete* at this point — §15 demonstrates that post-derivation comparison is admissible by doing one (Koide). A short, clearly-typed comparison passage (or table) after the diagnostic decimals — measured values quoted with uncertainties, differences stated, epistemic status typed exactly as §15 types Koide ("post-derivation consistency comparison, not blind prediction") — would give the reader the paper's strongest empirical fact instead of asking them to fetch it. As published, the manuscript's most striking number (the sub-ppm muon agreement) is literally never stated. (Candidate CI-9, persuasiveness.)

**Strongest passages.** §14.2's pointwise determination of ℬ; the closing scope paragraph (not an absolute electron prediction; middle output not blind; "These limitations do not alter the exact derivation of the ratios") — this paragraph is the paper's credibility at its best.
