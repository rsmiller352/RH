# Introduction Review

## The structural fact
**The manuscript has no introduction.** After the abstract, §1 opens directly into foundational ontology ("Section 1 begins with the simplest possible starting point: There is something rather than nothing."). There is no section that:

- frames the charged-lepton mass-ratio problem (its status as unexplained Standard Model input; the history of attempted mass relations; where Koide sits);
- situates the work relative to any literature (the reference list contains two items: the CODATA table and Koide 1983);
- explains to a physicist *why a closure-first derivation is being attempted* as a strategy (§1.6's "Overall Program" covers the internal logic but speaks the theory's own language);
- previews the derivational architecture end-to-end (the reader learns the route by walking it; the first appearance of "lepton" after the abstract is §12's preamble region — for eleven sections the physics target is out of sight);
- prepares the reader for the title's "observer-rooted asymmetry" (never assembled in the body — see OBSERVER_LANGUAGE_AUDIT);
- states the electron-calibration and Koide-validation roles up front in the body text (both are abstract-only until §§14–15).

Measured against the charter's introduction checklist, the manuscript scores: problem framing — absent; why closure-first — internal-facing only; I = 1 as precise axiom — **yes, §1.1 does this well**; difference from empirical fitting — deferred to §§13–15 (well handled there); architecture preview — partial (§1.6, abstract); observer-rooted preparation — absent; electron/Koide roles — abstract only; credible scope — **yes** (§1.6 and the falsifiability paragraphs are excellent).

## Why this matters
A foundational-physics editor reads: title → abstract → first two pages → references. On that path this manuscript shows: an unconventional title (fine), an honest but dense abstract missing its problem statement, an opening that reads as philosophy of ontology, and a two-item reference list. Every one of those is individually defensible; jointly they pattern-match to the crank profile *despite the content being disciplined* — the exact desk-rejection failure mode the charter warns about (substance sound, presentation triggers). The single highest-leverage structural repair available to this manuscript is a 1.5–2.5 page Introduction (numbered §1, shifting current §1 to §2, or as an unnumbered opening) containing:

1. **The problem** (2–3 paragraphs): charged-lepton masses as free parameters; the empirical Koide observation and its unexplained status; the methodological difficulty that most mass-relation proposals are fits or postdictions. ~6–10 citations to the standard literature (lepton mass relations, Koide follow-ups, discrete/relational foundations) would normalize the reference list without diluting the paper.
2. **The strategy** (1–2 paragraphs): what "closure-first" means operationally — derive dimensionless structure from an admission discipline; let geometry, runtime, and observer readout be forced constructions; admit exactly one empirical scale at the end. State here, in one sentence each, the electron-calibration role and the Koide-validation role.
3. **The roadmap** (1 paragraph + optionally the paper's single most valuable missing figure: a chain diagram I = 1 → arithmetic → FCC/RD → Ω → spectrum → 206/409 → ratios → calibration → Koide, with section numbers).
4. **The title, assembled** (2–3 sentences): the asymmetry is readout asymmetry, rooted in the observer's occupation of the reference role; what is intrinsic vs observer-relative.
5. **Scope and claim strength** (1 paragraph): can largely be moved/adapted from existing §1.6 and the abstract's caveats — the manuscript already owns this material.

## What must be preserved
§1.1's axiom statement and the falsifiability discipline must not be diluted by the new introduction; the introduction should *point into* them, not replace them. The paper's existing refusal to win by rhetoric is an asset — the introduction should frame stakes, not inflate claims.

## Verdict
The absence of an introduction is a **Level 4 (submission risk)** structural gap by the charter's own severity scale — not because anything present is wrong, but because the absent apparatus is what carries a foundational paper past editorial triage. It is also the natural home for four other repairs (problem statement, roadmap figure, title assembly, literature base), making it the most efficient single revision in the prioritized plan.
